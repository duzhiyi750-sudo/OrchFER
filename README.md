Abstract
Facial expression recognition (FER) infers human affective states from facial visual signals. Expression representation learning is fundamentally grounded in modeling raw visual observations. However, reliable expression perception remains constrained by both complex observation conditions and individual differences: complex observation conditions may lead to the loss of critical expression cues, while stable individual appearance can become entangled with dynamic expression information, thereby weakening the discriminability of expression representations and their cross-subject generalization ability. To address these issues, this study proposes a novel orchestration relationship learning framework (OrchFER) for video-based FER that provides a new perspective for understanding facial expression formation. The proposed framework is built on two key findings: orchestral coordination relationship and affective orchestration relationship. Accordingly, OrchFER consists of two dedicated modules: an expressive representation purification (ERP) module and an orchestration relationship mining (ORM) module. ERP disentangles facial deformation information from appearance-related characteristics to capture intrinsic expressive cues. ORM models the progressive formation of facial expressions through orchestral segments and exploits segment semantic orchestration to enhance high-level affective understanding. Extensive experiments are conducted on four widely used video-based FER datasets, namely, MMI, Oulu-CASIA, DFEW, and FERV39K. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves strong, consistent performance in constrained and in-the-wild scenarios. The source code will be available at https://github.com/duzhiyi750-sudo/OrchFER.
Keywords: facial expression recognition, orchestration relationship, expressive representation purification, computer vision, affective computing.
